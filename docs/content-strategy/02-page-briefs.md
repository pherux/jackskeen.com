# Page-Level Content Briefs

## How to use these briefs

Each brief defines the page’s job and evidence requirements. Section labels are
architecture, not approved marketing copy. Drafting must preserve visible approval
markers until the relevant entries in `05-approval-register.md` are resolved.

## Homepage `/`

**Job:** Establish relevance in ten seconds, introduce the success/fulfillment tension,
make The Roadmap the primary path, establish Jack’s credibility, and offer routes into
the knowledge library.

**Primary audience:** Accomplished leaders and referred prospects.

**Required narrative:**

1. Hero: who Jack helps, the tension, and Start Your Roadmap.
2. Problem: external success can coexist with uncertainty or dissatisfaction.
3. Solution: concise definition of The Roadmap.
4. Process: assessments, perspectives, conversations, patterns, final Roadmap.
5. Outcomes: only approved, supportable language.
6. Featured client story: one approved story with visible attribution.
7. Jack: short verified authority block linking to About.
8. Featured insights: real CMS records.
9. Podcast/video: real media records and transcripts where available.
10. Final CTA and complete footer.

**Proof required:** Verified biography, one approved client story, permitted image/video,
and final CTA destination.

**SEO intent:** Jack Skeen entity/home page; not a keyword landing page.

## The Roadmap `/roadmap`

**Job:** Explain the flagship offer completely enough for a qualified visitor to decide
whether to start a conversation.

**Primary audience:** Accomplished executives, founders, and professionals considering
a consequential next chapter.

**Source-backed material available:** Current Roadmap page and FAQ describe assessments,
stakeholder interviews, direct conversations with Jack, analysis, a personalized report,
and a process lasting roughly a couple of months. All details require confirmation.

**Required narrative:**

1. Clear definition of The Roadmap.
2. Who it is for and who it is not for.
3. The problem of seeing one’s own patterns without outside perspective.
4. Jack’s approved definition of “your genius.”
5. Process overview.
6. Assessments: names, purpose, and limits.
7. Stakeholder perspective: selection, consent, and format.
8. Conversations with Jack: number, length, and format.
9. Pattern analysis: what Jack synthesizes.
10. Final deliverable: contents and how it is used.
11. Approved outcomes without guarantees.
12. Approved stories.
13. Verified credibility.
14. FAQ.
15. Investment and qualification only if approved.
16. Start Your Roadmap CTA.

**Proof required:** Assessment names/rights, process specification, deliverable sample or
redacted visual, approved testimonials, verified credentials, current price, availability,
and payment policy.

**SEO intent:** Branded explanation of The Roadmap and answer page for “What is a
personal Roadmap?”

**Job:** Browse the retained article library without reproducing WordPress tags.

### Legacy root-level article routes

**Job:** Preserve valuable URLs and authorship while moving the body to typed CMS content.

**Required content:** Breadcrumb, topic, title, deck, Jack’s author profile, original date,

## Topic hubs `/insights/topics/[slug]`

**Job:** Become original, indexable explanations of the eight core ideas rather than thin
archives.

**Required for every topic:** 150–400 words of original approved editorial introduction,
Jack’s perspective, “start here,” one featured article, related articles/podcast/videos,
related topics, and a contextual Roadmap CTA.

**Writing dependency:** Jack must supply or approve the perspective for each hub. Article
lists alone are not sufficient for indexation.
